# Is This Posting Real

Back to [[Start Here]] · Related: [[Common Knockouts]]

## What it is

A method for checking that a job posting is real **and still open** before you spend time tailoring a resume for it.

## Why it matters

Job boards and search results are full of postings that closed weeks ago, were copied from somewhere else, or never existed. A list that looks like ten openings can turn out to be two. Checking first saves hours, and it protects you from scams.

## Leads versus proof

**Leads** are where you *find* postings: LinkedIn, Indeed, Handshake, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, job-alert emails, new-grad lists on GitHub, Reddit, and search-result snippets. They're useful, but they're never proof.

**Proof** is the posting page itself, opened today, on one of these:

1. The employer's own careers site.
2. The employer's applicant tracking system (the software companies use to collect applications), such as Workday (`*.myworkdayjobs.com`), Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters, Taleo or Oracle.
3. USAJOBS (`usajobs.gov`) for U.S. federal jobs.

## The check

Open the posting on the employer's site and confirm all four:

- [ ] The job title and the employer's name
- [ ] A location you can work in (or "remote" that allows your state)
- [ ] An Apply button or a working application link
- [ ] No sign that it's closed

Many careers sites load their job lists with JavaScript; give the page a few seconds before deciding it's empty.

## Signs a posting is closed

- "No longer accepting applications," "This job is closed," "Position filled," or "Job not found"
- The link sends you to a general search page or the careers home page
- A 404 or error page
- A "last date to apply" that has passed
- The Apply button is missing or greyed out
- Searching the employer's own site for the job ID returns nothing

## Signs of a scam

- You're asked to pay for anything: training, equipment, a background check.
- The "interview" happens only by text or a chat app, with no video or phone call.
- They ask for your Social Security number or bank details before a written offer.
- The email comes from a free address (like Gmail) instead of the company's domain.
- The offer comes almost instantly, for a job you barely applied to, at an unusually high salary.

If something feels off, find the company's careers page yourself (don't use the link you were sent) and look for the job there.

## Habits

- A check is good for the day you make it. Postings close without warning, so apply soon.
- Keep a short list of employers to watch even when they have nothing open, and set job alerts on their careers sites.
- When you can't open a posting (a sign-in wall or a bot check), treat it as unverified, not as real.
