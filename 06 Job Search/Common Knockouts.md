# Common Knockouts

Back to [[Start Here]] · Related: [[Is This Posting Real]]

## What it is

A **knockout** is a requirement that rules you out no matter how good the rest of your application is. Many applicant tracking systems ask knockout questions on the form and reject automatically if you answer "no."

## Why it matters

Reading a posting for knockouts **before** tailoring a resume is the cheapest time-saver in a job search. The checks below take two minutes.

## The six to check

### 1. Location and relocation

- Is the job in a place you can live and work? Does it require relocating?
- "Remote" may still be limited to certain states. Look for a list of allowed states or a line like "must reside in."
- Hybrid roles expect you near the office a few days a week.

### 2. Clearance and citizenship

- **"Active clearance required"** means you must already hold a U.S. government security clearance. For most students that's a knockout.
- **"Ability to obtain a clearance"** is different: the employer sponsors it after hiring. It usually requires U.S. citizenship.
- Some roles are limited to U.S. citizens or permanent residents by law (export-control rules, government contracts).

### 3. Years of experience

- "0–2 years" and "entry level" fit new graduates.
- "2+ years" is often flexible; internships and relevant part-time work can count. Apply if you meet most of the rest.
- "3–5+ years" or "senior" usually means it's not an entry-level role.

### 4. Start date and graduation date

- New-grad programs list the graduation window they accept (for example, "graduating between December and June").
- Some start dates are fixed. A program starting before you graduate is a knockout.

### 5. Internship enrollment

- Many internships require you to **still be enrolled** after the internship ends, so you can return to school. A posting for a summer after your graduation that says "must be returning to school" rules out graduating seniors, unless it says recent graduates are accepted.
- Read the "expected graduation" line closely; it's the most common internship knockout.

### 6. Required certifications

- Some roles, especially in cybersecurity and government contracting, require a certification on day one, such as CompTIA Security+.
- "Preferred" isn't required. "Required within 6 months of hire" is often fine.

## How to use this

For each posting: check these six, note anything borderline ("2+ years," "ability to obtain"), and decide **apply**, **apply with caveats**, or **skip** before writing a single bullet. A warm connection at the company can be a reason to apply despite a borderline flag; it's never a reason to ignore a hard knockout.
